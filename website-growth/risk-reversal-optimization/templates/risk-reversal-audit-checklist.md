# Risk Reversal & Guarantee Audit Checklist

Use this audit template to evaluate any website's current risk-reversal framework, score its effectiveness, and construct an optimized plan to neutralize purchase anxiety.

---

## Part 1: Diagnostic Scoring Framework

Rate the current on-site experience against each of the five pillars of effective risk reversal.

### Scoring Rubric
- **0 Points:** Completely missing, buried, or uses hostile legal language.
- **1 Point:** Present but hidden below the fold, or contains high friction / asterisk exclusions.
- **2 Points:** Prominent, clearly visible, written in simple, persuasive language, and placed next to critical CTAs.

---

### Pillar 1: Visual Visibility & Exposure
*Are your guarantees and return policies highly visible, or are they hidden in footer links?*
- [ ] **Home Page Exposure:** A trust bar or banner highlights guarantees/returns within the first two scroll screens. (Score: `__` / 2)
- [ ] **PDP Buy-Box Integration:** A guarantee badge, return window statement, or warranty link is placed within 50px of the "Add to Cart" button. (Score: `__` / 2)
- [ ] **Cart Drawer Integration:** Reassurance microcopy or a security guarantee seal is visible in the cart, directly adjacent to the Checkout button. (Score: `__` / 2)
- [ ] **Checkout Page Integration:** The payment page displays an explicit trust/guarantee statement adjacent to the credit card input fields. (Score: `__` / 2)

### Pillar 2: Copy Tone & Persuasion
*Do your policies read like a helpful customer success guide, or an aggressive legal defense document?*
- [ ] **Clarity vs. Legalese:** The main guarantee description is written in simple, conversational language (e.g., "Love it or your money back" instead of "Refunds subject to Article 4.2"). (Score: `__` / 2)
- [ ] **Asterisks & Exclusions:** There are no confusing asterisks (`*`) or hidden exclusions (e.g., "Only valid for unopened items") that undermine the primary promise. (Score: `__` / 2)
- [ ] **Customer-First Framing:** The copy emphasizes the buyer's comfort and confidence rather than the company's protection. (Score: `__` / 2)

### Pillar 3: Policy Generosity & Competitiveness
*Does your policy feel generous enough to turn fence-sitters into buyers, or does it feel restrictive and stingy?*
- [ ] **Return Window Length:** The return window is at least 30 days (ideally 60 or 90 days for physical goods to leverage the Endowment Effect). (Score: `__` / 2)
- [ ] **Return Shipping Cost:** For fashion, sizing-dependent, or high-Consideration goods, the brand provides free return shipping (prepaid labels). (Score: `__` / 2)
- [ ] **Restocking Fees:** There are no restocking fees or processing fees charged to the customer for returns. (Score: `__` / 2)

### Pillar 4: Friction Reduction
*How hard is it for a user to execute the policy if they are unhappy?*
- [ ] **Self-Serve Returns:** Customers can initiate a return or exchange online in a self-serve portal (e.g., Loop Returns) without having to call or write a long support email. (Score: `__` / 2)
- [ ] **Receipt Requirements:** No requirement to present a physical paper receipt (purchases should be looked up automatically via email or order number). (Score: `__` / 2)
- [ ] **"No Questions Asked" Execution:** The guarantee does not require the customer to "prove" why they are unhappy before receiving a refund or replacement. (Score: `__` / 2)

### Pillar 5: Technical Proximity & Tooltips
*Are you putting the right information at the exact place where anxiety peaks?*
- [ ] **Interactive Tooltips:** Guarantee badges on PDPs are interactive (hover or click reveal) so users can read the policy summary without leaving the product page. (Score: `__` / 2)
- [ ] **Micro-FAQ near Credit Card:** The checkout page has a micro-FAQ answering high-intent questions (e.g., "When will I be charged?", "How do I cancel?") directly near the checkout form. (Score: `__` / 2)

---

### Total Score Calculation
Sum your points across all checklist items:

| Total Score | Level of Friction | Business Risk | Priority Actions |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0 to 12** | **Critical Anxiety Gate** | High cart abandonment and low first-time buyer conversion. The site actively triggers customer suspicion. | **High Emergency:** Immediately move guarantees from the footer to the PDP buy box; rewrite all legalistic copy to customer-first phrasing. |
| **13 to 22** | **Moderate Friction** | Leaking potential revenue. Guarantees exist but are either buried, overly complicated, or contain hidden fees. | **Medium Priority:** Remove restocking fees, extend the return window, and add interactive tooltips to PDP badges. |
| **23 to 30** | **High-Trust / Optimized** | Industry-leading trust framework. Risk is fully reversed, driving optimal conversion performance. | **Maintenance:** Continuously monitor return rates against the math model and split-test larger return windows. |

### 2. Identify the Key Anxieties & Doubts triggered by the raw policy:
- *Anxiety 1 (Time limit stress):* "14 days is too short. What if I'm busy and miss the window?"
- *Anxiety 2 (Financial penalty):* "If I return it, I lose money on return shipping and restocking fees."
- *Anxiety 3 (Opening fear):* "How do I know if I like it without opening the box? This is a trap."

### 3. Draft the Persuasive, Optimized Policy:
- **Core Value Hook (Simple, big promise):** `[e.g., Love it or your money back. Risk-free 60-Day Trial.]`
- **Body Reassurance (Explain the process simply):** `[e.g., Try it at home. If it's not perfect, we'll provide a prepaid label and refund every penny. No restocking fees, no questions asked.]`
- **Friction-Free Call to Action (CTA):** `[e.g., Add to Cart — Try Risk-Free]`
